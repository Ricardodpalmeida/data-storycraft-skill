## What I assumed

You are presenting live to the CFO and want a decision. The Big Idea in one sentence: Pro churn more than doubled after the June price increase and has not come back down, so we recommend rolling the increase back for existing customers, and we need your approval.

Churn in the file is a monthly rate. The jump shows up in July, not June, so I assumed the increase took effect late in June or at renewal and the effect starts the following month. The chart shows June still at 2.5% and marks the increase with a dashed line, so it does not claim more than the data does. Confirm the effective date before the meeting, because the CFO will notice the one-month gap.

Pro is orange because it is the bad number. Basic and Enterprise are grey and act as the comparison. Everything is in English because you wrote in English.

## What the chart shows

Pro churn averaged 2.3% a month from January to June and 5.7% from July to December, about 2.5 times higher. It peaked at 6.1% in September and was still 5.8% in December, so it has plateaued high rather than faded. Basic and Enterprise did not move at all (4.2% and 0.9% on average in both halves). That comparison is the strongest evidence in the file that this is about Pro pricing and not general churn, so I kept both lines in, in grey.

The eye lands first on the thick orange line where it climbs right after the dashed June line, because it is the only colored and the heaviest element on the page.

## What to put on the slide

```
Roll back the June Pro price increase for existing customers

Pro churn has been above 5% a month since August. The Pro base
is down 22% since June (1,950 to 1,512 customers).

[chart PNG]

[Your revenue line, see below]
```

The slide title is the ask, and the chart's own title is the evidence, so they do not repeat each other. Read in order, title, one sentence, chart, they tell the whole story without you. Leave everything else off the slide. No table, no per-plan numbers beyond what the chart labels, no methodology. If someone asks, you have it in your head or in an appendix.

## What to say (about 40 seconds)

"Since the June price increase on Pro, monthly churn has gone from 2.3% to 5.7% on average, and six months later it has not come back down. Basic and Enterprise, which did not change price, did not move, so this is specific to Pro. Pro now churns faster than Basic, our cheapest plan. The Pro base went from 1,950 customers in June to 1,512 in December. I recommend we roll the price back for existing customers, and I am asking you to approve that today."

## What the CFO will ask, and what the file cannot answer

Revenue. The file has no prices, so I cannot tell you whether the increase paid for itself. This is the question that decides the meeting, and it is a quick calculation. 1,950 divided by 1,512 is 1.29. Unless the Pro price rose by more than 29%, Pro monthly revenue is now below its June level, assuming June was billed at the old price. If it did rise by more than that, lead with lifetime value and the fact that churn is still running near 6% (5.8% in December) instead of the revenue story. A second way to put it: at the January to June rate, Pro would have lost roughly 360 fewer customers between July and December (my estimate, assuming churn applies to the previous month's active base). Multiply that by your average Pro revenue per customer. Put whichever version works for you on the bottom line of the slide, or delete the line and have it ready to say.

What else changed. The Basic and Enterprise comparison is strong but it does not rule out something else hitting Pro in June or July, such as a competitor move, a product change or a billing issue. Check that nothing did, so you can say so.

Existing versus new customers. The file does not split them. If churn is measured on the prior month's base, the active customer counts imply new Pro sign-ups fell from about 70 a month (February to June) to about 19 in December. If that holds up against your sign-up data, a rollback for existing customers fixes only part of the problem, and the CFO may ask about the price for new customers. I kept this off the slide because it comes from my derivation and not from a column in the file.
