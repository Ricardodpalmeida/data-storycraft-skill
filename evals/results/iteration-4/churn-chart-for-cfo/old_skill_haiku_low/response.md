Slide text
Title: Pro churn averaged 5.7% a month after the June price increase, up from 2.3%
Three points for the slide:
- Basic (4.2%) and Enterprise (0.9%) churn did not change between H1 and H2 2025.
- Pro churn stepped up in July and has stayed between 4.8% and 6.1% since.
- About 345 more Pro customers churned from July to December than the January-June pace would have produced. Pro active customers fell from 1,950 in June to 1,512 in December.
Ask: Roll back the June increase for existing Pro customers, and track churn monthly against the January-June baseline of 2.3%.

Where the eye lands first: the blue Pro line jumping after the dashed June marker.

Assumptions I made
- The June price increase applied to the Pro plan only and took effect in June 2025. The file has no price data, so the timing is my reading of the dashed line.
- Churn is the monthly rate, and I averaged the six months on each side of June for the headline. The 345 figure is an estimate: the excess rate over 2.3% applied to each month's active Pro count.

Caveats to say out loud before the CFO asks
- This shows timing, not proof of cause. Anything else that changed for Pro in July (product, billing, a competitor, a campaign) would look the same in this data. Check that before the meeting.
- The rollback claim is a prediction. A cleaner ask is to roll back for a random half of existing Pro customers for one or two billing cycles and compare churn. That either confirms the effect or saves a full rollback if it does not.
- The data says nothing about new Pro customers, so the slide should only recommend the rollback for existing customers, as you asked.
