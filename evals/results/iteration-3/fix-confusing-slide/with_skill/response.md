## What is wrong with the old slide

Biggest problem: the slide makes two unrelated points and buries yours, because the revenue bars and the rep line sit on two truncated axes, so the chart cannot show that revenue stayed flat while reps grew 45%.

| Lesson | Issue | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Context | "Market Overview 2025" is a topic, not a message. A market share pie sits next to a reps vs revenue chart, and the data runs from Q1 2024 to Q4 2025 under a 2025 title. | Leadership has to decide for themselves what to look at. That is the confusion your director felt. | One message per slide. Market share is off the slide and the title states the finding. |
| Visual | Five-slice pie, exploded and shadowed. Revenue as bars on an axis that starts at 4.0, reps as a line on a second axis that starts at 37.5. | The Q1 24 bar looks one sixth as tall as Q4 24 when the real gap is 12%. With two truncated axes, where the line crosses the bars is an accident of axis choice, and a sharp viewer will discard the argument. | No pie, no second axis, no truncated bars. Both series are lines on one axis, indexed to Q1 2024 = 100. |
| Clutter | Dashed gridlines in both directions, two boxed legends (the last reps point runs into one), 45 degree labels, shadow, explosion. | Every element costs effort and none of it carries the message. | All removed. Direct labels replace the legends and all text is horizontal. |
| Focus | Eight bar colors that encode nothing, five pie colors, a green line. The eye lands first on the exploded orange Supplier B slice, which is a competitor. | When everything is colored, nothing stands out, and the loudest element contradicts your point. | Grey base with one blue accent on revenue. Markers and labels only on the start, the peak and the end. |
| Design | Centered titles, no takeaway, no source, a 14 by 6 canvas. | The slide cannot be understood from its title alone. | Left-aligned action title, titled axes, source footnote, 16:9 canvas. |
| Story | No tension, no resolution, no ask. | The room hears numbers but not a conclusion. | The title carries the finding and a shaded window carries the sting. The ask belongs on your closing slide. |

## The redesign

One line chart, both series indexed to Q1 2024 = 100 so they share an axis. Revenue is the thick blue line and reps are the grey line. The title reads "Reps are up 45% since Q1 2024, but revenue is up only 2%". The absolute values (38 reps and $4.1M at the start, 44 reps and $4.6M at the Q4 24 peak, 55 reps and $4.2M at the end) are labeled on the chart, so nothing is lost by indexing. A light grey window marks "Since the Q4 24 revenue peak: reps up 25%, revenue down 9%".

The eye lands first on the title, then on the thick blue line peeling away from the grey one, which is the gap you want leadership to see.

Every number in the title and annotations is computed from the data in the script, so they cannot drift from the chart.

## Assumptions I made

- I assumed the ask is to pause hiring until the cause is known, since you did not give one. It is in the Big Idea above but not on the slide, because this slide's job is the evidence. Change it if your ask is different.
- I removed the market share pie. It tells a different story and does not support yours. If leadership wants it, give it its own slide or an appendix slide as sorted horizontal bars with "Us" in the accent color (B 31%, A 24%, Us 20%, C 14%, D 11%).
- I wrote "up only 2%" instead of "has not grown". Revenue went from $4.1M to $4.2M, which is growth, and during 2024 it rose 12% while reps rose 16%. Someone in the room will notice that. The stronger and fully defensible fact is the post-peak one: 11 more reps and revenue down 9%.
- The footnote says "internal sales data". Replace it with the real system of record by editing the `SOURCE` line at the top of the script.

## What you will be asked

The chart shows timing, not cause. Expect the ramp-up objection, because 5 of the 11 reps added since the peak joined in the last two quarters. Have revenue per rep by hire cohort or tenure ready as backup. The aggregate data here cannot answer that. A useful number for your talk track is that revenue per rep fell from about $108K to $76K per quarter (down 29%). It is deliberately not on the slide, so the slide keeps one message.
